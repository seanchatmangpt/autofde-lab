\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Autonomous agents must allocate finite resources across exploration
options, and every classical policy family (greedy, $\varepsilon$-greedy,
UCB1, PUCT) optimizes performance while remaining structurally incapable
of governance: outputs are non-deterministic across platforms,
non-conserving under discretization, silently degrading under failure,
and invisible to audit. This dissertation develops, in full formal
detail, the mathematics of the alternative demonstrated by the AutoFDE
artifact chain: allocation as a \emph{certified, receipted, host-invariant
artifact}.

The mathematical contributions are layered. \emph{Algebra}: the escort
cascade is developed through its partition function $Z(q)=\sum_i m_i^q$,
R\'enyi dimensions $D_q$, and the Legendre duality between the lens
exponent $q$ and the local H\"older spectrum $f(\alpha)$; the
substitution of integer-lens powers $m^q$, $q\in\Z$, for the
transcendental exponential $e^{\tau S}$ is proved to algebraize
determinism---bit-identical replay across CPython and BEAM hosts becomes
a theorem about the dyadic grid $\dyad_{16}=\Z/2^{16}$ rather than a
hope about floating point. \emph{Geometry}: allocations live in the
simplex $\simplex^{N-1}$; canonical hash-ordering exhibits the allocator
as a function on the orbit space $\simplex^{N-1}/\sym_N$; the compiled
policy is measured to have the exactly-uniform symmetric fixed point, to
\emph{fund rarity} (a near-zero-mass candidate receives $0.1721$, more
than the minimum strong share $0.1183$, via the rare lens $q=-1$), and
to compress four orders of magnitude of mass ratio into a factor
$3.4$ of share ratio. A conjectured coverage floor of
$\Lambda_0/N = 5/32$ is \emph{refuted by measurement} (raw weak share
$0.0837$), and the refutation is retained as a first-class result: the
$\rho$-routed, multiplicatively-weighted cascade is not a barycenter of
its lenses, and its sharp floor is stated as an open problem.
\emph{Calculus}: the lens derivative $\frac{d}{dq}\log
Z(q)=\E_{\escort{q}}[\log m]$ and the Gibbs identity
$\frac{d\Ent}{d\tau}=-\tau\Var(S)$ are proved; the sum--product law of
combinatorial maximalism is formalized (option graphs grow with the sum
of alternatives while path cardinalities carry their product), giving
the information-theoretic reason governance must preserve option
entropy rather than maximize point yield. The phase transition itself
is given quantitative form: per-implementation governance has integrity
$(1-p)^H$, exponential in hosts $H$, while the single-kernel law
replaces it with a fixture of constant cost---the availability
argument of the transistor, transposed from components to hosts.

The dissertation's method is its own subject: a falsification ledger in
which the circular benchmark, the every-softmax ``theorems,'' the
notational equivalence proof, the local reference implementation, and
most recently the coverage-floor conjecture were each deleted or refuted
with commit-anchored receipts. What survives is the governance envelope
and its phase-change structure, argued with explicit disanalogies and
falsifiers.
\end{abstract}

\chapter{Introduction}

\section{The problem}

Let an autonomous agent face a finite frontier of candidate branches
$c_1,\dots,c_N$, each characterized by option entropy $\Omega_i \ge 0$,
historical yield $Y_i$, and cost $C_i > 0$, and let it hold a discrete
budget $B=(B_{\mathrm{tick}}, B_{\mathrm{mem}}, B_{\mathrm{depth}},
B_{\mathrm{risk}})$. The allocation problem is the map
\begin{equation}
A:\; (\text{features}, B) \;\longmapsto\; \shares \in \simplex^{N-1}
\;\longmapsto\; \Z^N \cap B\!\cdot\!\simplex^{N-1},
\label{eq:allocation-problem}
\end{equation}
from real-valued evidence to a point of the probability simplex and
thence, by discretization, to integer allocations. Every classical
policy instantiates \eqref{eq:allocation-problem}; none of them makes
the \emph{decision artifact}---the output $\shares$ and its integer
projection---auditable, replayable, or conserved. This dissertation
develops the mathematics of the ones that do, and argues that doing so
is a phase transition in the sense of Chapter~\ref{ch:phase-formal}.

\section{The thesis statement}

\begin{principle}[Thesis statement]
When the map~\eqref{eq:allocation-problem} is manufactured as a single
certified artifact---deterministic across hosts
(Ch.~\ref{ch:arithmetic}), conserving by construction
(Ch.~\ref{ch:geometry}), refusing by type rather than degrading
(Ch.~\ref{ch:envelope-summary}), and emitting content-bound receipts
into process event data (Ch.~\ref{ch:conformance})---the failure class
of divergent, silent, unverifiable implementation is removed
structurally, and composition across hosts and trust boundaries becomes
arithmetically possible rather than merely permitted.
\end{principle}

\chapter{Background and Related Work}
\label{ch:background}

\paragraph{Allocation under uncertainty.}
UCB1's logarithmic regret~\cite{auer2002}, PUCT search~\cite{rosin2011},
and the general theory~\cite{sutton2018} characterize the performance
frontier under a trust model in which the policy's implementor is
trusted absolutely. This dissertation inverts the trust model and asks
what the decision artifact must satisfy for third-party replay.

\paragraph{Information geometry and multifractal analysis.}
The escort distributions $\escort{q}(i) \propto m_i^q$ originate in the
multifractal formalism~\cite{halsey1986,renyi1970} and in nonextensive
statistics~\cite{tsallis1988}; their geometric home is Amari's
$\alpha$-geometry~\cite{amari2000}, which we connect to the lens
parameter in \S\ref{sec:alpha-geometry}. Entropy variational principles
follow Jaynes~\cite{jaynes1957} and Shannon~\cite{shannon1948};
elementary facts follow Cover and Thomas~\cite{cover2006}.

\paragraph{Apportionment.}
Discretizing quotas onto integer budgets is the classical
apportionment problem; the failures of naive rounding methods are
catalogued by Balinski and Young~\cite{balinski1982}. The floor
projection of Chapter~\ref{ch:geometry} is deliberately the simplest
member of this family, and its theorems are the corresponding weakest
(hence most robust) guarantees.

\paragraph{Lattice geometry.}
Counting lattice points in dilated polytopes is Ehrhart theory; the
standard reference is Beck and Robins~\cite{beck2007}.

\paragraph{Floating point.}
IEEE~754 specifies correct rounding for $+,-,\times,\div$ and leaves
transcendental functions to implementations; the consequences are
classical~\cite{goldberg1991,ieee754}. Chapter~\ref{ch:arithmetic} turns
this negative fact into the positive design decision that makes
bit-identity purchasable.

\paragraph{Object-centric process mining.}
OCEL~2.0~\cite{ocel2,pm4py} and object-centric process
querying~\cite{ocpq} supply the durable event substrate into which
receipts are projected (Chapter~\ref{ch:conformance}).

\paragraph{The industrial analogues.}
Landes~\cite{landes1969}, Rosenberg~\cite{rosenberg1976},
Mokyr~\cite{mokyr1990} establish the constraint-class-removal model of
general-purpose transitions; Bardeen and Brattain~\cite{bardeen1948}
and Moore~\cite{moore1965} supply the transistor instance;
MacKenzie~\cite{mackenzie2001} the sociology of mechanized trust.

\chapter{The Geometry of Governed Allocation}
\label{ch:geometry}

\section{The simplex, the symmetric group, and the orbit space}

Fix the compiled shape $N \le 8$. Write
\begin{equation}
\simplex^{N-1} \;=\; \Bigl\{\, \shares=(s_1,\dots,s_N)\in\R_{\ge 0}^N
\;:\; \textstyle\sum_i s_i = 1 \,\Bigr\}
\end{equation}
for the standard simplex, a compact convex polytope with $N$ vertices
$e_1,\dots,e_N$. The symmetric group $\sym_N$ acts on
$\simplex^{N-1}$ by permuting coordinates,
$\sigma\cdot(s_1,\dots,s_N) = (s_{\sigma^{-1}(1)},\dots)$, and the
physically meaningful state of an \emph{unordered} frontier is a point
of the orbit space
\begin{equation}
\qquad \simplex^{N-1}/\sym_N \;=\; \bigl\{\, \text{nonincreasing
rearrangements} \,\bigr\} \;=\; \bigl\{\, s_1\ge s_2 \ge \dots \ge
s_N\ge 0,\ \sum s_i = 1 \,\bigr\},
\end{equation}
the \emph{Weyl chamber} of type $A_{N-1}$: a closed simplex glued from
$N!/2$ fundamental domains along reflection walls.

\begin{definitionx}[Canonical section]
\label{def:canonical-section}
The \emph{canonical hash section} is the map $\kappa$ assigning to each
candidate $c_i$ the digest $\kappa(c_i) =
\mathrm{SHA\text{-}256}(\mathrm{blob}(c_i))$ and ordering candidates by
$(\kappa(c_i), c_i)$ lexicographically; the induced section
$\sigma_\kappa : \simplex^{N-1}/\sym_N \to \simplex^{N-1}$ selects, in
each orbit, the representative whose coordinates are indexed by
canonical order.
\end{definitionx}

\begin{proposition}[Determinism as descent to the quotient]
\label{prop:descent}
Suppose the measure map $M: \mathcal{C}^N \to \simplex^{N-1}$ satisfies
$M(c_{\sigma(1)},\dots,c_{\sigma(N)}) = \sigma\cdot
M(c_1,\dots,c_N)$ ($\sym_N$-equivariance) and is computed by
arithmetic that is exact on its value grid. Then
$M \circ \sigma_\kappa$ is a well-defined function on the orbit space,
and identical inputs yield bit-identical outputs.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equivariance means $M$ is constant on orbits up to the action; the
section selects a unique representative; exactness on a totally ordered
grid removes implementation latitude. Uniqueness of the representative
forces uniqueness of the output bytes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remarkx}
Proposition~\ref{prop:descent} is the formal content of the
``canonical sort'' engineering rule: sorting by hash is not a
convenience but the choice of a measurable section for a group action,
and replay determinism is the statement that the composite descends.
The golden fixture of \S\ref{sec:golden} is its executed proof.
\end{remarkx}

\section{The budget polytope and its lattice geometry}

Discrete allocations live in the dilated simplex
\begin{equation}
B\!\cdot\!\simplex^{N-1} \;=\; \bigl\{\, x\in\R_{\ge0}^N : \textstyle\sum_i
x_i \le B \,\bigr\}, \qquad B\in\N,
\end{equation}
and the feasible integer allocations are the lattice points
$B\!\cdot\!\simplex^{N-1}\cap\Z^N$.

\begin{proposition}[Ehrhart count of the budget simplex]
\label{prop:ehrhart}
$\bigl|B\!\cdot\!\simplex^{N-1}\cap\Z^N\bigr| = \binom{B+N}{N}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Lattice points are weak compositions $x_1+\dots+x_N+x_{N+1}=B$ in $N+1$
nonnegative parts (the slack variable absorbs the inequality); the count
is stars and bars, the degree-$N$ Ehrhart polynomial of the simplex.
\end{proof}

\begin{remarkx}[Maximalism, first appearance]
The policy does not and must not enumerate
$\binom{B+N}{N}\sim B^N/N!$ allocations (for $B=9973$, $N=8$ this
exceeds $10^{28}$); it selects one point of this lattice by a
deterministic rule. Chapter~\ref{ch:dfcm} elevates the discipline:
\emph{bounds, not enumerations}.
\end{remarkx}

\section{The floor projection: apportionment theorems}

Let $\shares\in\simplex^{N-1}$ with active set
$A=\{i: s_i\ge\theta\}$, $\sum_{i\in A}s_i=1$ after renormalization.
The deployed projection assigns $\floor{s_i B}$ of each discrete
budget dimension.

\begin{theorem}[Floor apportionment]
\label{thm:floor}
The map $\Phi_B:\shares\mapsto(\floor{s_iB})_{i\in A}$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(Feasibility)} $\sum_{i\in A}\floor{s_iB}\le B$;
\item[(ii)] \emph{(Residual bound)}
$B-\sum_{i\in A}\floor{s_iB} = \sum_{i\in A}\{s_iB\} < |A|$;
\item[(iii)] \emph{(Monotonicity)} $s_i\ge s_j \Rightarrow
\floor{s_iB}\ge\floor{s_jB}$;
\item[(iv)] \emph{($\sym_N$-equivariance)}
$\Phi_B(\sigma\cdot\shares)=\sigma\cdot\Phi_B(\shares)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) Superadditivity of floor:
$\sum\floor{s_iB}\le\floor{\sum s_iB}=\floor{B}=B$.
(ii) $B-\sum\floor{s_iB}=\sum\bigl(s_iB-\floor{s_iB}\bigr)
=\sum\{s_iB\}$, a sum of $|A|$ fractional parts.
(iii) $x\mapsto\floor{xB}$ is nondecreasing.
(iv) Coordinatewise application commutes with index permutation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remarkx}[Relation to classical apportionment]
The Hamilton (largest-remainder) method redistributes the residual of
(ii) to the largest fractional parts, achieving $\sum=B$ exactly; it
pays with the Alabama paradox and population-monotonicity
failures~\cite{balinski1982}. The deployed projection keeps the weaker
guarantees (i)--(iv)---which are invariant under every monotone
transformation of the inputs---and documents the residual rather than
redistributing it. In the certified kernel the same discipline appears
as \emph{one-sided} truncation: saturating fixed-point division and
multiplication lose value, never manufacture it, so flow conservation
residuals are nonnegative by construction and were additionally
\emph{measured} across adversarial fixtures before being claimed.
\end{remarkx}

\chapter{The Escort Cascade: Algebra and Multifractal Calculus}
\label{ch:escort}

\section{Masses and the partition function}

Let each candidate carry masses
$\mass^{(k)}=(m^{(k)}_1,\dots,m^{(k)}_N)\in\R_{\ge0}^N$ on $K=4$
measure axes (option entropy, inverse cost, yield, salience
\eqref{eq:salience-def} below). For a lens $q\in\R$ define the
\emph{partition function}
\begin{equation}
Z_k(q) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{N} \bigl(m^{(k)}_i\bigr)^{q},
\qquad q\in\R,
\end{equation}
finite for $q\ge 0$; for $q<0$ it is defined iff all masses are
positive (zero mass under a negative lens is a typed refusal, mirroring
the kernel's $\mathrm{ZeroMassUnderNegativeLens}$).

\begin{equation}
\escort{q}^{(k)}(i) \;=\; \frac{\bigl(m^{(k)}_i\bigr)^{q}}{Z_k(q)}.
\label{eq:escort-def}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Limiting lenses]
$\escort{0}$ is the uniform distribution (sibling-coverage convention:
zero-mass siblings included); $\escort{1}$ is proportional allocation;
as $q\to+\infty$, $\escort{q}\to e_{i^\ast}$ with
$i^\ast=\arg\max m_i$ (exploitation); as $q\to-\infty$,
$\escort{q}\to e_{i_\ast}$ with $i_\ast=\arg\min_{m_i>0} m_i$
(rarity). The compiled vocabulary $q\in\{2,1,0,-1\}$ samples all four
regimes with one exponent each.
\end{proposition}

\section{R\'enyi dimensions and the lens calculus}

The R\'enyi entropy of order $q$ is
$D_q(\mass) = \frac{1}{1-q}\log Z(q)$ (with $D_1$ the Shannon entropy
by limit), and the multifractal scaling exponent is $\tau(q) =
(q-1)D_q = \log Z(q) - \log Z(1)$ when $\sum m_i = 1$.

\begin{proposition}[Lens derivative identity]
\label{prop:lens-derivative}
For $q$ in the interior of the convergence region and positive masses,
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dq}\log Z(q) \;=\; \E_{\escort{q}}\!\left[\log m\right],
\qquad
\frac{d^2}{dq^2}\log Z(q) \;=\; \Var_{\escort{q}}(\log m)\;\ge\;0 .
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate $\log Z = \log\sum m_i^q$:
$\frac{d}{dq}\log Z = \frac{\sum m_i^q\log m_i}{Z} =
\E_{\escort{q}}[\log m]$. The second derivative is the variance of
$\log m$ under $\escort{q}$ (standard exponential-family identity;
$\log Z$ is convex in $q$ by H\"older).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Monotone concentration]
Along the lens line, increasing $q$ moves escort mass toward larger
$\log m$ at a rate equal to its escort variance: the lens parameter is
a concentration knob whose sensitivity is exactly the current
dispersion of $\log m$. This is the quantitative form of the
``exploitation'' direction.
\end{corollary}

\section{The Gibbs identity for the deleted reference engine}

The deleted softmax was $p_i(\tau)\propto e^{\tau S_i}$ with salience
\begin{equation}
S_i \;=\; \frac{\Omega_i\,Y_i}{\max(C_i,\varepsilon)}.
\label{eq:salience-def}
\end{equation}
We record for the ledger's historical chapter the identity that was
once presented as a theorem:

\begin{proposition}[Gibbs entropy identity]
\label{prop:gibbs}
For $p(\tau)\propto e^{\tau S}$ on a finite frontier,
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\Ent}{d\tau}\bigl(p(\tau)\bigr) \;=\; -\,\tau\,
\Var_{p(\tau)}(S) \;\le\; 0 \quad (\tau\ge 0),
\end{equation}
with $\Ent(p)=-\sum_ip_i\log p_i$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\frac{dp_i}{d\tau}=p_i\bigl(S_i-\E[S]\bigr)$; hence
$\frac{d\Ent}{d\tau} = -\sum_i \frac{dp_i}{d\tau}(1+\log p_i) =
-\sum_i p_i\bigl(S_i-\E[S]\bigr)\log p_i$; substituting
$\log p_i = \tau S_i - \log Z$ and expanding gives
$-\tau\Var(S)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remarkx}[Ledger entry]
Proposition~\ref{prop:gibbs} holds for \emph{every} Gibbs distribution
over \emph{any} score vector; it is an exponential-family fact, not a
property of salience~\eqref{eq:salience-def}. It is printed here once,
proved, and marked: the earlier presentation of such identities as
characteristic theorems of the allocator was an audit finding
(Ch.~\ref{ch:ledger}).
\end{remarkx}

\section{Legendre duality: the H\"older spectrum}

Multifractal analysis pairs the lens line with the spectrum of local
exponents. Define, for $\alpha\in\R$,
\begin{equation}
f(\alpha) \;=\; \inf_{q\in\R}\,\bigl\{\, q\alpha - \tau(q) \,\bigr\},
\qquad \alpha(q) = \frac{d\tau}{dq} = \E_{\escort{q}}[\log m] + 1
\end{equation}
(the additive constant absorbs normalization). Then $f$ is the Legendre--Fenchel
transform of $\tau$; $f(\alpha)$ is the fractal dimension of the set of
sites with local exponent $\alpha$, and $\tau$ is recovered by the
inverse transform. For the finite frontier, $f$ is concave, supported
on $[\log m_{\min},\log m_{\max}]$.

\begin{remarkx}[Why this matters for policy]
A lens policy $(q_0,\dots,q_D)$ per cascade level is a \emph{sampling
of the $\tau$-curve}. The Legendre picture says which local structures
each lens sees: $q>1$ sees the large-mass peaks ($\alpha$ small), $q<1$
sees the rare support ($\alpha$ large), $q=0$ sees everything
uniformly. A compiled vocabulary $\{2,1,0,-1\}$ is a four-point
quadrature of the entire multifractal spectrum---which is why
Chapter~\ref{ch:blend} finds the blended policy flat \emph{and}
rarity-funding at once: the quadrature includes both endpoints.
\end{remarkx}

\section{Information geometry of the escort family}
\label{sec:alpha-geometry}

The map $q\mapsto\escort{q}$ traces a curve in
$\simplex^{N-1}_{>0}$ through the statistical manifold of the frontier.
Under the Fisher metric
$g_{ij}=\E_{\escort{q}}\bigl[(\log m_i)(\log m_j)\bigr]-\E[\log
m_i]\E[\log m_j]$ (the variance structure of
Proposition~\ref{prop:lens-derivative}), the escort curve is a
geodesic up to reparameterization of Amari's
$\alpha$-family with $\alpha$ affine in $q$~\cite{amari2000,tsallis1988}:
the lens line \emph{is} the $\alpha$-geometry of the frontier,
traversed at unit speed proportional to escort variance. We do not fix
the affine convention (authors differ); the structural statement is
convention-free.

\begin{remarkx}
This is the precise sense in which the deleted softmax and the kept
escort cascade are ``the same mathematics'': both traverse the same
geodesic, one with continuous parameter $\tau$ (requiring $e^{\tau S}$,
a transcendental), one with integer parameter $q$ (requiring only
products $m^q$). Chapter~\ref{ch:arithmetic} proves that this
difference in arithmetic is the entire difference in governability.
\end{remarkx}

\chapter{The Compiled Policy: Emergent Geometry of the Certified Blend}
\label{ch:blend}

The deployed measure is not a single lens but the certified kernel's
blend over $K=4$ measures and $Q=4$ lenses with the compiled constants
of Appendix~\ref{app:constants}: lens registry
$\mathbf{q}=(2,1,0,-1)$ and mixture matrix
\begin{equation}
\Lambda \;=\;
\begin{pmatrix}
\tfrac25 & \tfrac3{10} & \tfrac15 & \tfrac1{10}\\[2pt]
\tfrac1{10} & \tfrac15 & \tfrac3{10} & \tfrac25\\[2pt]
\tfrac14 & \tfrac14 & \tfrac14 & \tfrac14\\[2pt]
\tfrac12 & 0 & \tfrac12 & 0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \sum_{q}\Lambda_{kq}=1\ \ \forall k,
\qquad \mathrm{ETA}=\tfrac12 .
\label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
The kernel documents, and its correspondence tests pin, the blend
identity
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{allocate} \;=\; \sum_{k,q}\,\Lambda_{kq}\cdot
\mathrm{allocate\_single\_lens}(k,q),
\label{eq:blend-identity}
\end{equation}
where each $\mathrm{allocate\_single\_lens}$ is itself a full cascade
under lens $q$: two-path flow routing with per-node split $\rho_v$,
multiplicatively-weighted routing updates, and saturating Q16.16
arithmetic throughout. The interaction of these mechanisms makes the
blend \emph{emergent}: it is not a barycenter of textbook escorts, and
this chapter is the mathematical record of what that means---including
one conjecture that died for it.

\section{The symmetric fixed point}

\begin{proposition}[Uniformity at symmetry]
\label{prop:uniform}
When all $N$ candidates present identical feature vectors, the kernel
returns $s_i = 1/N$ exactly. \emph{Measured}: for $N=8$ and identical
axes, each raw share is $0.125$, exactly representable in Q16.16
($8192/65536$), with raw total mass $1.0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Verification status]
Symmetry of inputs forces every per-lens cascade to a permutation-
invariant output; uniqueness of the fixed point follows from the
deterministic integer arithmetic; the uniform vector is the only
permutation-invariant distribution. Executed numerically to exactness;
a formal proof against the Rust source is future work
(\S\ref{sec:open-floor}).
\end{proof}

\section{A conjectured coverage floor, refuted}

The natural first model of the blend is the \emph{naive barycenter}:
ignore routing and weights, treat each lens term as its pure escort
$\pi_{kq}$, and read \eqref{eq:blend-identity} as
$s=\sum\Lambda_{kq}\pi_{kq}$. Since every $\pi_{k,0}$ (coverage) is
uniform over $N$, this model predicts a floor:

\begin{conjecture}[Naive coverage floor]
\label{conj:floor}
$s_i \;\ge\; \frac{\Lambda_0}{N}$ for every candidate $i$, where
$\Lambda_0=\sum_k\Lambda_{k,0} = \tfrac15+\tfrac3{10}+\tfrac14+\tfrac12
= \tfrac54$; for $N=8$, $s_i\ge 5/32 = 0.15625$.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{theorem}[Refutation]
Conjecture~\ref{conj:floor} is false for the deployed kernel.
\emph{Measured}: with three real candidates
$(5.0,\,0.1,\,0.8,\,0.4)$, $(3.0,\,0.1,\,0.7,\,0.21)$,
$(0.01,\,0.01,\,0.01,\,10^{-6})$ and five phantom pads, the dead-end
raw share is $0.083679 < 5/32$, and the raw total mass is $0.4962$,
not $\sum_{k,q}\Lambda_{kq}=4$: the phantom candidates absorb half the
mass, and the blend is not the naive barycenter.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remarkx}[What the refutation teaches]
The $\rho$-routing and multiplicative-weight machinery couple the
lenses: the coverage component's uniform baseline is \emph{routed}
through the same flow splits as every other component and does not
survive as an additive floor. The refutation is retained, per ledger
discipline, as a first-class result: it delimits exactly which
abstraction of the certified kernel is faithful (the blend identity
\eqref{eq:blend-identity} as an internal correspondence) and which is
not (componentwise escort additivity).
\end{remarkx}

\begin{openproblem}[Sharp floor of the certified cascade]
\label{sec:open-floor}
Derive $\inf_{\mass}\, s_i(\mass)$ over frontiers with $m_i\ge\varepsilon$,
as a function of $(\Lambda,\mathbf{q},\rho,\mathrm{ETA})$, or prove no
positive uniform floor exists once routing is coupled. A formalization
of the kernel in a proof assistant, from which
Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform} and the floor both follow, is the natural
instrument.
\end{openproblem}

\section{Rarity funding: the measured anti-starvation mechanism}

\begin{modelx}[Rarity funding, measured]
With $N=8$ real candidates, seven at axes $(1.0,0.1,0.5,0.5)$ and one
near-zero at $(0.001,0.001,0.001,10^{-9})$: the \emph{weak} candidate
receives raw share $0.1721$, exceeding the minimum \emph{strong} share
$0.1183$. With one dominant candidate
$(10,1,2,20)$ against seven weak ones, all weak candidates receive
$0.0958$ and the spike $0.3292$: four orders of magnitude of mass
ratio compress to a share ratio of $3.4$.
\end{modelx}

The mechanism is the rare lens $q=-1$: under
\eqref{eq:escort-def}, $m^{-1}$ amplifies small masses, so the blend's
rare component concentrates on the weakest candidate---the compiled
policy's built-in, quantitative anti-starvation. Together with extreme
compression this gives the blend its measured character: \emph{flat in
the middle, protective at the bottom, compressed at the top}---a
log-scale policy, matching the Legendre reading of
\S\ref{sec:alpha-geometry} (the quadrature includes both endpoints of
the H\"older spectrum).

\section{A decision-theoretic reading (model)}

\begin{modelx}[Bayes hedging]
Let the planner hold a Dirichlet posterior $\pi$ over which branch is
ultimately viable, with full support. Any policy assigning $s_i=0$
forges the option value $\E_\pi[v_i\mathbf{1}\{i\text{
optimal}\}]>0$. A policy with the measured floor behavior hedges at
posterior-dependent cost; the flatness the performance lens reads as
waste, the governance lens reads as the price of never being
categorically wrong. This is a model of the \emph{value} of the
measured geometry, not a theorem about the kernel: the posterior, the
value process, and the loss are assumptions.
\end{modelx}

\chapter{Arithmetic Determinism: Dyadic Rationals and the Transcendental Boundary}
\label{ch:arithmetic}

\section{The dyadic grid}

Q16.16 arithmetic computes over the nonnegative dyadic grid
\begin{equation}
\dyad_{16} \;=\; \bigl\{\, k\,2^{-16} \;:\; k\in\{0,\dots,2^{32}-1\}
\,\bigr\} \;\subset\; \Q,
\end{equation}
with saturating $+,-,\times$ and truncating $\div$ (floor toward zero).
Every value is an exact rational; every operation is a total function
on a finite totally ordered set; every result is bit-stable.

\section{What IEEE 754 does and does not promise}

IEEE~754~\cite{ieee754} specifies correctly-rounded $+,-,\times,\div$
and (since 2008) $\sqrt{\,}$; it does \emph{not} specify
transcendental functions, which may and do differ across
libraries~\cite{goldberg1991}. A softmax requires $\exp$; therefore a
float softmax is not bit-portable in principle, only
platform-coincident in practice. This is not a tuning problem; it is a
classification problem: $\exp$ is transcendental, and no rounding rule
makes its implementations agree.

\section{The algebraization theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Algebraization of the exponential]
\label{thm:algebraization}
Let $m=e^{S}$ with $S$ in the additive group generated by the feature
logs. The escort family $\{\escort{q}\}_{q\in\R}$ restricts on the
integer sublattice $q\in\Z$ to maps computed from $\mass$ by finite
products and one exact division:
$\escort{q}(i)=m_i^{q}/\sum_jm_j^{q}$, $m_i^q$ by $|q|$ saturating
multiplications (and one reciprocal for $q<0$). Consequently, on
$\dyad_{16}$, every integer-lens escort is an algebraic function of
the inputs---no transcendental is evaluated anywhere in the
measure---whereas every non-integer lens (equivalently, every Gibbs
softmax at $\tau\notin\{0,1\}$ relative to the log-mass grid) requires
one.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from $m^q$ for integer $q$ being a finite product; division
is specified exact-by-truncation; saturation is a monotone clamp into
$\dyad_{16}$. The converse holds because $m^q=e^{qS}$ with $qS$
leaving the log-grid almost surely forces evaluation of $e^x$ at a
non-grid point.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bit-identity]
\label{thm:bit-identity}
Any two faithful implementations of the same integer-lens cascade on
the same dyadic grid produce identical output bits for identical
inputs. Hence host- and language-independence of outputs is a
structural property of the arithmetic, not a testing accident.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each operation is a specified total function on a finite set;
composition of specified total functions is a specified total
function; there is no latitude for an implementation to differ.
\end{proof}

\chapter{Conformance Geometry and Adversarial Separation}
\label{ch:conformance}

\section{Event data as typed multigraphs}

An OCEL~2.0 log is a typed multigraph $G=(E\sqcup O, \ell)$ with events
$E$, objects $O$, and typed incidence $\ell\subseteq
E\times\mathrm{Qual}\times O$~\cite{ocel2}. Receipted allocations are
events bound to plan/branch/world objects; the log is the durable
state of the governance process.

\section{Alignments as geodesics}

Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a process model (e.g.\ a discovered Petri net) and
$\sigma$ a trace. The synchronization product $\mathcal{S}(\sigma,
\mathcal{M})$ is the weighted graph on pairs (log step, model step);
the alignment cost
\begin{equation}
d(\sigma,\mathcal{M}) \;=\; \min_{\pi\in\Pi(\sigma,\mathcal{M})}
\;\sum_{e\in\pi} w(e)
\end{equation}
is the shortest-path (geodesic) distance in $\mathcal{S}$, computed by
$A^\ast$ search~\cite{pm4py}. Conformance is the statement
$d\le\delta$; fitness the normalized ratio.

\section{The counterfactual battery as adversarial neighborhood}

Let $\mathcal{L}\subset\mathrm{Traces}$ be the lawful language and
$\mu:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}$ a mutator (premature actuation,
unadmitted injection, skipped teardown, subtask inversion, crossed
object links, allocation inversion). Governance holds when the detectors
separate the mutant neighborhood from the lawful set:

\begin{equation}
\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{L}}\; d\bigl(\mu(\sigma),\,\mathcal{M}\bigr)
\;\ge\; \delta_\mu \;>\; \sup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{L}}
d(\sigma,\mathcal{M}) .
\label{eq:separation}
\end{equation}

Condition \eqref{eq:separation} is the metric form of fail-closed: a
margin, not a verdict. The declared limitation stands: the mutator set
is imagined by the same authors as the detectors
(Ch.~\ref{ch:ledger}), so \eqref{eq:separation} is verified on a
designed neighborhood, and the margin against unimagined adversaries is
unknown.

\chapter{Combinatorial Maximalism: The Sum--Product Law of Option Preservation}
\label{ch:dfcm}

\section{The sum--product law}

Let a decision configuration be a graph whose nodes carry option sets
$O_1,\dots,O_n$.

\begin{principle}[Sum--product law]
The configuration space grows with the \emph{sum} of alternatives,
$|G|=\Theta(\sum_i|O_i|)$, while the path space carries their
\emph{product}, $|\Pi|=\prod_i|O_i|$. A governed reasoner may hold all
of $G$ and must never enumerate $\Pi$: selection among preserved
options proceeds by bounds and sections, with every truncation
declared.
\end{principle}

The Ehrhart count of Proposition~\ref{prop:ehrhart} is the arithmetic
instance: the budget polytope's $\binom{B+N}{N}$ lattice points are
bounded, never listed. The escort cascade is the dynamical instance:
$N$ candidates are all retained as support of $\escort{q}$, and the
lens line selects within them by a one-parameter family rather than by
pruning to a singleton.

\section{Option entropy as log-volume}

Define the option state of a frontier as the pair
$(\mathrm{supp}(\shares), \Ent(\shares))$. Since
$\Ent(\shares)\le\log|\mathrm{supp}(\shares)|$ with equality iff
uniform, entropy is the log-volume of the effective option set: the
maximalist objective is precisely to keep log-volume of lawful
alternatives bounded away from zero. Anti-starvation is the support
invariant $|\mathrm{supp}|\ge1$ (projection law) and, in the compiled
policy, the measured rarity-funding floor (\S\ref{ch:blend}): the
two-level guarantee that the reasoner is never left with the empty
product.

\section{Pareto exposure, not argmax}

An argmax returns one point of $\simplex^{N-1}$: a decision with no
residual structure. A governed allocation returns a point \emph{with}
its section (ordering), its lattice projection
(Theorem~\ref{thm:floor}), its residual (declared), and its
receipt---a full fiber of audit structure over the decision.
Formally, the artifact exposes the map
\begin{equation}
\shares \;\longmapsto\; \bigl(\sigma_\kappa(\shares),\,
\Phi_B(\shares),\, B-\textstyle\sum\Phi_B(\shares),\,
\mathrm{SHA256}(\mathrm{canon}(\shares))\bigr),
\end{equation}
and every consumer composes with this exposure rather than with
$\shares$ alone. This is the sum--product law at the interface level:
the exposed structure grows additively with the laws enforced, while
the space of compatible consumers grows multiplicatively.

\section{Reversibility lattice}

Options carry a partial order $a\sqsubseteq b$ (``$a$ is recoverable
from $b$'s consequences''). The design rule---$\mathrm{COMPENSATABLE}
\neq \mathrm{REVERSIBLE}$, and unknown reversibility is a fence---is
the statement that the lattice's order structure, not its labels, is
the decision input: preference goes to the change preserving the most
lawful upper bounds.

\chapter{The Phase Transition, Formally}
\label{ch:phase-formal}

\section{Availability functionals}

\begin{modelx}[Integrity of multi-host agreement]
\label{model:availability}
Fix a per-host, per-change divergence probability $p$ (platform
arithmetic, version drift, silent fallback) and $H$ hosts that must
agree for an audit to pass. Per-implementation governance has integrity
\begin{equation}
I_{\mathrm{multi}}(H) \;=\; (1-p)^H \;\xrightarrow{H\to\infty}\; 0
\qquad\text{(exponentially)}, 
\end{equation}
while the single-kernel law replaces the $H$-fold conjunction with one
fixture of constant cost: $I_{\mathrm{kernel}}(H) =
\mathbf{1}[\text{fixtures pass}]$, independent of $H$. This is the
tube-reliability argument $r^n$ of integrated electronics, transposed
from components to hosts. It is a model: $p$ is an assumed parameter,
and independence across hosts is an assumption.
\end{modelx}

\section{Failure-class removal as language emptiness}

Let $\mathcal{B}(S)$ be the set of observable behaviors of system $S$.
Old regime: there exist executions in $\Phi_{\mathrm{silent}}$ (silent
fallback), $\Phi_{\mathrm{diverge}}$ (cross-host disagreement),
$\Phi_{\mathrm{unreceipted}}$ (actuation without content-bound trace),
and these sets are nonempty and reached with positive probability.
New regime: the executed language satisfies
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{B}(S)\;\cap\;\bigl(\Phi_{\mathrm{silent}}\cup
\Phi_{\mathrm{diverge}}\cup\Phi_{\mathrm{unreceipted}}\bigr)
\;=\;\varnothing ,
\end{equation}
by construction: refusals are typed, transport equality is pinned by
fixture, receipts are content-bound. Removal, not mitigation: the
transition of Chapter~\ref{ch:phase} in set-theoretic form.

\section{The order parameter}

Define $\mu(t)$ = fraction of delivered product-surface lines that are
hand-written rather than manufactured. The operating contract imposes
the monotone descent law $\mu(t)\le\mu(t-\Delta)$ per milestone; the
session documented here moved $\mu$ discontinuously (the reference
measure deleted outright), and the ledger
(Ch.~\ref{ch:ledger}) is the trajectory of $\mu$ with receipts. In the
variational analogy, $\mu$ plays free energy: every audit step must
lower it, and no step may trade a temporary increase for a promised
future decrease without a paydown plan committed in the same change
(Jaynes' maximum-entropy calculus applied to the codebase: the
macrostate is the law surface; the microstates are its lawful
renderings~\cite{jaynes1957}).

\chapter{The Phase-Change Analysis}
\label{ch:phase}

\section{A model of phase change}

A technology undergoes a phase change when jointly: (1) a constraint
\emph{class} of the old regime is removed structurally
(\S\ref{ch:phase-formal}); (2) composition unlocks---assemblies of
scale the old regime cannot reach at any price, because old failure
rates grew with assembly size (Model~\ref{model:availability});
(3) a standard interface compounds improvement effort (steam:
standardized power and threads; transistor: the planar process; here:
the committed artifact with its provenance tripwires).

\subsection{Steam}
Early engines were thermally poor and economic only at the minehead;
the phase change was removal of the biological/geographical power
class, the factory system as composition unlock, and standardization
as the compounding interface~\cite{landes1969,rosenberg1976}.

\subsection{The transistor}
At demonstration, not faster than tubes; the initial win was removal
of the filament failure class, making $r^n$ assemblies
arithmetically feasible, with integration as the compounding
interface~\cite{bardeen1948,moore1965}.

\section{Disanalogies, stated}

\textbf{Scale}: one laboratory, not an economy; mechanism, not
history. \textbf{Binding constraint}: that auditability (not
performance) binds consequential autonomy is a prediction, not a
measurement. \textbf{Size of kernel}: $N\le 8$ with a compiled lens

\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

The mathematics of this dissertation is the shadow of a deletion. When
the local softmax died, what remained was structure: a simplex and its
orbit space, a dyadic grid and its algebra, an escort line and its
Legendre dual, a blend whose floor is open, a fixture whose equality
is exact, and a ledger that records the conjectures killed in passing.
The phase-change claim is now the precise statement that removing the
implementation failure class converts host-count from an exponential
liability into a constant, and the honest statement that everything
else---adoption, scale, history---is future work with named
falsifiers. Governance is not a property an artifact has; it is a
property of the process allowed to modify the artifact. This
dissertation has tried to be an instance of its own theory about it.
